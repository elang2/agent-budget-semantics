Here's the run data, later than I said, and the headline changed on re-measurement so I'd rather
you have the corrected version than the one I described.

**What changed.** I had this as "one framework's enforcement fires and its own loop ignores it".
That was wrong, and the fault was in my harness rather than the framework. The mock never read
`tool_choice`, so it was a non-compliant provider, and I'd attributed its non-compliance to the
agent. Re-ran every cell against two provider behaviours with the predictions committed first.

What the data supports now is a three-way split rather than enforced/not-enforced, and the
distinction is how much a limit depends on the counterparty:

- **Stops locally, signals nothing.** openai-agents 0.22.0, `stopped_by: budget`, 3 model and 3
  tool calls, and it sends `tool_choice` zero times under either provider.
- **Withdraws the tool declaration, then doesn't execute a call offered anyway.** semantic-kernel
  1.44.1. Its fourth request carries no `tools` at all. The non-compliant provider hands it a tool
  call regardless, and it reports 3 executed against 4 offered.
- **Asks the provider to stop, executes what comes back.** agno 1.2.5 sets `tool_choice="none"` at
  the limit (`agno/models/base.py:886` and `:997`) with no client-side refusal, so against the
  non-compliant provider it reached 9 tool calls across 10 model calls under a declared
  *tool-call* limit of 3. Against the honouring provider it holds at 4 and 3.

**On the Agno row specifically, the upstream record is wider than my measurement and you should
have it.** The behaviour class — `tool_call_limit` not reliably terminating the loop — has five
open-or-closed issues from five different reporters, which is much stronger than my single run:
agno-agi/agno#8304 (open, ViniciusRomano), #9385 (open, TheHonestBob, against 2.8.7), #6984
(closed, bb2103), #10041 (open, zmin1217) and #7962 (open, denizmatsu, the counter resetting on
every stream invocation). A fix PR, #6993, was closed unmerged on 2026-04-06. **None of them is
the 1.2.5 mechanism I measured**, and none is mine — #9385 is a different version and reports
roughly the opposite failure, where Agno skips execution but keeps relaying a user-supplied forced
`tool_choice`. So treat the issues as evidence the parameter is contested and my cells as evidence
of one specific version's behaviour; conflating them would overstate both.

Against the honouring provider **all four meet their declared limit**. That's the part worth
keying vectors to: a well-behaved counterparty makes all three mechanisms look identical, so a
conformance check that only ever talks to a cooperative provider can't tell a limit that holds
from one that merely asks. Which is why your per-field vantage requirement is load-bearing and not
cosmetic — the count has to come from somewhere the enforcing component doesn't control.

**Five fields you should not key anything to, because no code produces them.** This is the part I
most want you to have before you cut vectors. In `results/S2-executed.json`,
`counter_at_budget_stop`, `unit_observed`, `mock_confirmed_calls`, `enforced` and crewai's
`stopped_by: "budget_then_finalize"` are all hand-authored. `counter_at_budget_stop` equals
`budget_value` in all seven enforcing rows and the file's own schema calls that tautological, and
`mock_confirmed_calls` equals `actual_llm_calls` in 8 of 8 rows. No runner reads any framework's
internal counter.

That last one is worth the detail, because `mock_confirmed_calls` looks like an independent
cross-check and for half the rows it is the same number twice. Traced through the runners, over the
eight executed rows: `actual_llm_calls` is `len(ledger)` for **four** — semantic-kernel, crewai,
llamaindex and agno — so for those `mock_confirmed_calls` is literally the same variable copied. It
is `budget_value` for openai-agents, which is a declared number and not a count of anything. Only
the remaining three, autogen, langchain and langgraph, read a count from the framework. And `actual_tool_calls` needs its own correction, because I was about to
recommend it to you on the wrong grounds. It **is** the better column — it counts executions
directly — but it is not a framework self-report either: in all eight runners it is
`tool_calls_observed`, a counter my harness increments inside each tool body. So on the LLM side
only autogen, langchain and langgraph carry a framework reading, and on the tool side none of them
do. The results file now carries a `field_provenance` block over a fixed vocabulary (`ledger`,
`harness_counter`, `framework_api`, `declared`, `modelled`, `hand_authored`) so you can query this
rather than take my paragraph for it, and a test forbids anything claiming `framework_api` that is
a harness counter or a declared constant.

**What is a reading.** `results/S2-toolchoice-2026-10-04.json` and the eight per-request ledgers
under `results/ledgers/`. Every numeric field there comes from a ledger or a runner result object,
and each field says which in `field_provenance`. The mechanism labels are derived by
`classify_mechanism()`, and each row now emits `classified_on` — the tuple it was classified on,
with the provenance of each element — so you can check the label against its inputs. Worth knowing
that two of those inputs are runner-side rather than ledger-side: the executed tool count is my
harness counter and `stopped_by` is the framework's own word. An earlier docstring claimed all the
signals were ledger reads, which was wrong.

**The scorecard, both ways, because the basis changes the answer.** The predictions name
`actual_llm_calls`/`actual_tool_calls`, which are framework-reported: on those columns 6 of 6
cells match. Against the ledger it's 5 of 6 — semantic-kernel was predicted 4/3 and the ledger
reads 4/4, which is the offered-versus-executed gap above. Same fact, two columns. I'd rather you
had both than the flattering one.

**Caveats, so you don't find them the hard way.** Six frameworks were installable at pin and I ran
four; LangGraph and LlamaIndex were left out for no reason I recorded at the time, which I can't
defend after the fact. CrewAI's two cells were added after the predictions, so they're exploratory
and I keep them out of the scorecard.

**The CrewAI cells have a dependency confound you should know about before keying anything to
them.** crewai 1.15.16 requires `openai>=2.30,<3`; openai-agents 0.22.0 requires `>=3,<4`. No
single environment satisfies both, and mine resolved `openai==3.8.0` — so the CrewAI cells ran
outside CrewAI's own declared range while every other framework was inside its range or
unconstrained. Each cell records the resolved version and that compliance flag. CrewAI's run
against the non-compliant provider also errors on a pydantic `TaskOutput.raw` validation. I have
been wrong twice about why. First I blamed my mock; then an openai v3 class rename, and the class
in question imports under openai 2.54.0, so it isn't a v3 change at all. **The cause is
not established.** What's checkable is the contrast in the tree: it errors against the
non-honouring provider and runs clean to 4/3 against the honouring one.

Run-to-run stability for these eight cells is measured, and both runs are committed so you can
check it rather than take the sentence: `results/replication/run-a.json` and `run-b.json` with
their ledgers. The comparison is a test, and its denominator is 8 cells, 136 cell fields, 8
ledgers and 36 ledger entries, with zero differences once `executed_at`, `request_id` and
`timestamp` are excluded. Two runs is not a distribution, and the S2/S4/S5 rows in the
`*-executed.json` files remain single runs.
